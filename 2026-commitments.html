Most life advice ranks activities by how good they feel. This ranks them by how much load a single commitment carries, and by what is left standing when it ends. Those two measures explain most of why some lives feel full and some feel merely busy.

The category

There is no settled English word for this class of thing, which is part of why nobody manages it deliberately. The nearest existing terms each catch one face.

Practices, in Alasdair MacIntyre's sense: cooperative activities with internal goods and standards you have to submit to. Right about craft and submission, silent on bundling. Ground projects, from Bernard Williams: the commitments that supply your reason to keep going, such that losing one is nearer to death than to disappointment. Right about load-bearing, silent on plurality. Need-satisfying pursuits, from self-determination theory: right about the mechanism, far too thin a list of needs.

All three miss the ratio. Something can be a practice, a ground project and need-satisfying and still be narrow. The useful unit is the commitment that supplies many unrelated goods from one decision and keeps supplying them after your enthusiasm runs out. The test is structural: remove it and see whether anything sags.

Five layers

Ordered by what happens when each goes to zero. The failure modes differ in kind, and people misdiagnose across layers constantly — treating a hollow life as though it were a dead one.

Three bands

An earlier version of this atlas scored everything on one scale, which quietly implied that a sabbatical is a weak congregation. It isn't — it's a different kind of object. Three bands, scored separately.

Three kinds of residue

A single residue score hid the distinction people actually use to choose.

Friendship upkeep and a solo craft both leave a lot behind, but they aren't the same leaving. Skill residue survives everything — it's portable, it's yours, and it's the only kind that moves cities with you. Relational residue lives in other people and can be lost to their deaths, their moves, or one bad year. Institutional residue outlives you: the thing exists whether or not you do, which is the only form of residue that continues working after you stop.

They aren't fungible. Losing a craft and losing a friendship are unlike losses, and a commitment that supplies only one kind is more fragile than its total suggests.

Eight dials

Two commitments can carry the same inputs and behave nothing alike. These are the axes that explain the difference — and the settings you can adjust when one isn't working.

The engine

Everything in the top half of the ledger shares one feature: specific other people are counting on you.

That is the whole mechanism. Load doesn't come from an activity being rich; it comes from obligation forcing continuation past the point where motivation dies, which is precisely when compounding starts. The solo version of nearly every item here carries half as much. Practising alone is craft. Playing in a band is craft plus four relationships plus a deadline plus an outcome you don't control. Traveling alone supplies novelty and autonomy; traveling with someone adds two of the heavy six.

The multiplier is always other people, and specifically people who will notice your absence. An audience won't do it. A class you paid for won't do it. It has to be the kind of absence that costs someone something.

Subtraction

A page about load-bearing structures with no account of demolition is incomplete. Adding is celebrated and removing is treated as failure, which is why most overloaded lives stay overloaded.

Anti-bundlers

Where discretionary money and attention actually go. None of these is forbidden. They are simply not doing the work people expect, and they scale badly — doubling the spend flattens the return rather than doubling it.
